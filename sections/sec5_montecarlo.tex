\section{Monte Carlo Evidence}
\label{sec:mc}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dependent_conformal_var} is asymptotic and its remainder is
conservative. This section measures what the correction delivers at sample sizes
a practitioner has, under misspecification of a known kind and severity, and
records one thing that does not converge.

\paragraph{Design} Returns follow $r_t = \sigma_t\varepsilon_t$ with a
GARCH$(1,1)$ recursion at $\omega = 10^{-5}$, $\alpha_1 = 0.10$,
$\beta_1 = 0.85$, so Corollary~\ref{cor:garch} applies to every cell. Five
innovation laws are used, in increasing order of tail departure: Normal, which is
correct specification; Student-$t(5)$; Student-$t(3)$; the skewed-$t(3,-0.5)$ of
\citet{hansen1994autoregressive}; and the mixture
$0.95\,\mathcal N(0,1)+0.05\,\mathcal N(0,25)$. All are standardised to unit
variance. The forecaster is given the \emph{true} GARCH parameters and assumes
Normal innovations, so the only quantity varying across the five processes is the
innovation law and the only defect is distributional.

Sample sizes are $T\in\{500,\,1000,\,2000,\,5000,\,10000\}$ with $f_c = 0.70$ and
$\alpha = 0.01$, giving calibration samples from 350 to 7{,}000 observations, and
each cell is replicated 500 times: 12{,}500 replications in total. The shift is
equation~\eqref{eq:qV} throughout.

Three readings follow from the upper panel of Table~\ref{tab:mc_grid}.

\emph{Coverage is restored across every process.} From $T = 1{,}000$ onward the
corrected violation rate lies within $\nMCCorPiMaxDev$ of nominal for all five processes,
including the skewed-$t$, whose population rate is $\nMCPopSkew$ --- more than twice the level
it reports. The residual falls with $T$ and there is no ordering of it by the
severity of the misspecification: a one-parameter shift removes as much of a
severe unconditional error as of a mild one.

\emph{$\qVstat$ converges to the population miscalibration, which does not vanish
with data.} Under correct specification it tends to zero, from $\nMCQvNormalOne$ at $T = 500$ to $\nMCQvNormalFive$ at $T = 10{,}000$. Under each misspecification it converges
instead to a non-zero limit, ordered by the severity of the tail departure:
$\nMCQvTFiveFive$ for Student-$t(5)$, $\nMCQvTThreeFive$ for Student-$t(3)$, $\nMCQvMixFive$ for the mixture, $\nMCQvSkewFive$ for the skewed-$t$. The quantity being estimated is a property
of the pair of laws, not an artefact of the sample.

\emph{Its variance falls at the parametric rate.} The replication standard
deviation of $\qVstat$ under correct specification falls from $\nMCSdNormalOne$ to $\nMCSdNormalFive$ across a twentyfold increase in $T$, a factor of $\nMCSdRatioNormal$ against $\sqrt{20} = \nMCSqrtTwenty$ (the ratio is computed from the unrounded replication standard deviations; dividing the two figures as printed gives $\nMCSdRatioNaive$). Lemma~\ref{lem:quantile_mixing} carries an additional
$\sqrt{\log n}$; over this range of $T$ the two rates are not separable, and no
attempt is made to separate them.

\paragraph{The small-sample cost of the finite-sample convention} At $T = 500$
the corrected rate is $\nMCCorPiSmallLo$ to $\nMCCorPiSmallHi$ across all five processes, including
the correctly specified one --- systematically conservative rather than nominal.
The mechanism is the conformal index of Section~\ref{sec:theory_lemmas}: at
$n = 350$ the level $\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ is $\nSupConfIndex$ rather than
$0.99$, an overshoot of $\nSupConfOvershoot$, and $\qVstat$ inherits it. Under correct
specification, where the population shift is zero, the overshoot is the entire
signal: mean $\qVstat$ runs $\nMCQvNormalOne,\,\nMCQvNormalTwo,\,\nMCQvNormalThree,\,\nMCQvNormalFour,\,\nMCQvNormalFive$
across the grid, against overshoots of $\nSupConfOvershoot,\,\nMCOverTwo,\,\nMCOverThree,\,\nMCOverFour,\,\nMCOverFive$.
The correction is upward-biased on a forecaster that needs no correction, by
construction rather than by misspecification, and the bias is $\mathcal O(1/n)$.
Section~\ref{sec:recal} reports the panel consequence.

\subsection{What does not converge}
\label{sec:mc_diverges}

The right-hand panel of Table~\ref{tab:mc_grid} reports the share of replications
whose \emph{raw} forecasts are classified green, and it does not behave like the
left-hand panel.

The behaviour has a limit, and it can be stated.

\begin{proposition}[Asymptotic content of the traffic-light classification]
	\label{prop:traffic_limit}
	Let a forecast series have long-run violation probability
	$\pi = \lim_{T}\E[\hat\pi_T]$, let $\hat\pi_T$ be the realised violation rate
	over a test block of length $n_T\to\infty$ with $\hat\pi_T\to\pi$ in
	probability, and let $\tau = 4/250$ be the Basel green-zone threshold on the
	scaled exceedance count \citep{basel1996}. If $\pi\neq\tau$, then
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:traffic_limit}
		\P(\text{green})
		\;=\;
		\P\!\left(\hat\pi_T\le\tau\right)
		\;\longrightarrow\;
		\mathbf 1\{\pi<\tau\}.
	\end{equation}
	The classification therefore identifies $\operatorname{sign}(\pi-\tau)$ in the
	limit and nothing else: two forecasters whose long-run violation rates lie on
	the same side of $\tau$ receive the same verdict with probability tending to
	one, however far apart those rates are and however far either is from the
	nominal $\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
	$\P(\hat\pi_T\le\tau)=\P(\hat\pi_T-\pi\le\tau-\pi)$, which tends to $1$ when
	$\tau-\pi>0$ and to $0$ when $\tau-\pi<0$ by convergence in probability. The
	excluded case $\pi=\tau$ is the one in which the limit is not degenerate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The interval the classification cannot resolve]
	\label{cor:traffic_interval}
	At $\alpha = 0.01$ the threshold is $\tau = \nTLTauOverAlpha\,\alpha$. Every forecast series
	whose reported threshold cuts off a long-run tail probability in
	$(0,\,\nTLTauOverAlpha\,\alpha)$ is classified green with probability tending to one.
	Under standardised Student-$t_5$ innovations, whose $1\%$ quantile is
	$\nGapQTrueStd$ in units of conditional scale, a series at the upper end of
	that interval reports a Value-at-Risk understated by $\nTLUndTFive$\%; under
	standardised $t_3$ the figure is $\nTLUndTThree$\% and under Normal innovations $\nTLUndNormal$\%.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Zone improvement is a sub-event of coverage improvement]
	\label{prop:zone_inclusion}
	Let $\hat\pi$ and $\hat\pi'$ be the realised exceedance rates of a forecast
	series before and after a correction, and let $Z(\cdot)$ be the traffic-light
	classification, a non-decreasing step function of the exceedance count whose
	boundaries lie at $\tau = \nTLTauOverAlpha\,\alpha$ and $\nTLTauYellowOverAlpha\,\alpha$, both strictly above
	nominal. If $Z(\hat\pi') < Z(\hat\pi)$ --- a zone upgrade --- and the
	correction does not carry the rate below $\alpha$ by more than its initial
	excess, then $|\hat\pi' - \alpha| < |\hat\pi - \alpha|$. The converse fails:
	$|\hat\pi' - \alpha| < |\hat\pi - \alpha|$ is compatible with
	$Z(\hat\pi') = Z(\hat\pi)$ for any movement that does not cross a boundary.
\end{proposition}

The proof is immediate. Every boundary of $Z$ sits above $\alpha$, so a downward
crossing begins strictly above nominal and ends nearer to it; and $Z$ is constant
between boundaries, so any movement interior to a zone is invisible to it. The
content is not the implication but its asymmetry, and that asymmetry is what
Proposition~\ref{prop:traffic_limit} predicts: an instrument that resolves
$\mathrm{sign}(\pi - \tau)$ can register a change of sign and nothing finer, so it
must undercount improvement and can never overcount it.

The panel is the measurement rather than an illustration. Across the
\nZoneCells{} model--asset pairs at $\alpha = 0.01$, the rolling correction
produces \nZoneUpgrades{} zone upgrades and moves \nZoneCloser{} pairs closer to
nominal. Every upgrade is among the latter: the count of pairs upgraded without
moving closer is \nZoneUpNotCloser{}. In the other direction \nZoneCloserNotUp{}
pairs move closer with no zone change at all --- a quarter of the improvement the
classification does not see. Zero counterexamples in \nZoneUpgrades{} trials is
not a strong correlation; it is the inclusion the proposition forces, visible in
the data without any further assumption.

This is the bridge between the limit statement above and the cost accounting of
Section~\ref{sec:recal}. A benefit measured in zone upgrades is a benefit
measured through a sign, and Section~\ref{sec:recal} therefore prices the
indication rule in the quantile score and keeps the zone count as the
administrative figure a supervisor reads.

Three features of Proposition~\ref{prop:traffic_limit} distinguish it from the
identification result of Section~\ref{sec:blindspot}. It requires no construction,
no restriction on the return law and no matched alternative --- only consistency
of a sample frequency. It does not shrink with evidence: the interval in
Corollary~\ref{cor:traffic_interval} is the same at every sample size, and what
grows with $T$ is the confidence with which a series inside it is passed. And it
applies even where the identification constraint does not bind, since it concerns
what the classification retains from the exceedance path rather than what that
path can distinguish.

Table~\ref{tab:mc_grid} is the finite-sample version. The green share equals the
share of replications with $\hat\pi\le\tau$ in all 25 cells exactly, and tracks
the normal approximation
$\Phi\!\left((\tau-\pi)/\mathrm{sd}(\hat\pi_T)\right)$ to within \nMCNormalApproxPP{} percentage points on average. The boundary case behaves as the proposition says it must: a
process with $\pi = \tau$ exactly gives a green share of $48$ to $55$\% at every
sample size from $T = 1{,}000$ to $T = 10{,}000$, converging to nothing.

The skewed-$t$ process, whose population rate of $\nMCPopSkew$ clears the boundary, is
driven out: $\nMCGrnSkewOne$\% green at $T = 500$ falls to $\nMCGrnSkewFive$\% at $T = 10{,}000$. The
Student-$t(5)$ process is not. Its population rate is $\nMCPopTFive$ --- a forecaster
understating its exceedance probability by half --- and it sits inside the
boundary, so the same concentration works the other way: $\nMCGrnTFiveOne$\% green at $T = 500$ rises to $\nMCGrnTFiveFive$\% at $T = 10{,}000$. Student-$t(3)$ at $\nMCPopTThree$ rises from $\nMCGrnTThreeOne$\% to $\nMCGrnTThreeFive$\%, and the mixture at $\nMCPopMix$ from $\nMCGrnMixOne$\% to $\nMCGrnMixFive$\%.

More data makes the traffic light more confident, and for three of the four
misspecified processes here it makes it more confidently green. What converges to
the miscoverage is $\qVstat$.

This is the identification constraint of Section~\ref{sec:blindspot} in a setting
where the truth is known by construction, and it is worth separating from a claim
the design does not support. The zone boundary is not miscalibrated: a forecaster at $\hat\pi = \nMCPopTFive$ genuinely produces fewer than four exceedances per
250 days more often than not, which is what the zone is defined to detect. The
point is narrower and is about what a practitioner may infer. A green zone
sustained over a longer record is evidence that the exceedance count is below the
boundary, and it is not evidence that the reported quantile is the quantile it
claims to be --- the two coincide only when the miscoverage is large enough to
cross the boundary. Waiting for more data does not close that gap; it widens the
share of replications in which the gap is invisible.

\subsection{What the harness can and cannot see}
\label{sec:mc_harness}

The design was gated on reproducing an existing two-sample-size study before any
new sample size was reported. It does, on all ten cells and all six reported
quantities, at a tolerance of three standard errors of the difference between two
independent Monte Carlo estimates. Two defects surfaced in the course of that
gate, both in the harness rather than in what it was measuring: a tolerance
computed as if the replication distribution of a $1\%$ order statistic were
normal, and a seed reused across processes. Both are stated in full, with what
the corrected gate still cannot see, in Supplement~S.18.
