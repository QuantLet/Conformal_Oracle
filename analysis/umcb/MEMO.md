# Phase 2 — q_V and the uMCB term of Gneiting & Resin (2023)

Generated by `analysis/umcb/run_umcb.py` over 240 model–asset pairs at alpha = 0.01.

## The decomposition as implemented

`S_raw = MCB - DSC + UNC` with the CORP recalibration done by pool-adjacent-violators using the weighted alpha-quantile as the pooling operation (the quantile-loss analogue of the mean). MCB is split into the part a constant shift can remove and the remainder:

    uMCB = S_raw - min_c S_raw(x - c)
    cMCB = MCB - uMCB

The minimising constant for quantile loss is the empirical (1-alpha)-quantile of the residuals x - y, which is what q_V estimates on the calibration split. A relation is therefore guaranteed by construction; the question is its form.

Decomposition identity check: max |S_raw - (MCB - DSC + UNC)| = 1.39e-17.

## Why the two are not interchangeable

A second-order expansion of the quantile loss gives uMCB ≈ ½·f·q_V², with f the residual density at the alpha-quantile. Solving for the implied f per pair (quantile-grid failures excluded, and the 1 pairs with numerically negative uMCB dropped):

- 5th-95th percentile of implied f: **0.2 to 18.7**, a factor of 83; median 3.5.

The same q_V therefore maps to score penalties differing by an order of magnitude depending on the asset's tail density. The two quantities are not readable off one another without f.

(The 1 negative uMCB values are numerical: the minimising constant is found by an interpolated quantile, so the achieved reduction can be ~1e-8 below zero. They are dropped here rather than clipped.)

# Verdict

**Essentially (a), and the paper should say so plainly.**

q̂_V is not a new statistic. For the quantile loss, the constant shift that
minimises the score is the empirical (1−α)-quantile of the residuals — the
population object q̂_V estimates. Gneiting & Resin's decomposition already
contains that shift's score consequence as the unconditional part of MCB. The
relation is not an empirical coincidence to be discovered; it is an identity up
to the estimation split and the conformal ceiling.

The measurements bear that out: Spearman correlation of |q̂_V| with uMCB is
**0.899** over all 240 pairs and **0.816** excluding the quantile-grid failures.

**What is *not* true is that one can be read off the other.** They live in
different units — q̂_V is a shift in return units, uMCB a difference in loss
units — and the map between them runs through the residual density at the
α-quantile. Backing that density out per pair gives a 5th–95th percentile range
of **0.2 to 18.7**, a factor of 83. Within Panel A the rank correlation falls to
**0.593**, with individual pairs moving more than 100 rank positions between the
two orderings (GARCH-N/ICLN is 104th by |q̂_V| and 1st by uMCB).

So the honest description is: **the same defect, measured in two units, with a
pair-specific exchange rate.** That is an interpretability argument, not a
novelty argument, and it must be written as one:

- **Cite Gneiting & Resin (2023)** at the point where q̂_V is introduced, and
  state that its score consequence is the unconditional miscalibration term of
  their decomposition.
- **Drop any claim of novelty for the statistic.** What survives is that a shift
  expressed in return units is directly comparable to the VaR it corrects, so
  R = |q̂_V|/|VaR| is a scale-free ratio a risk manager can read. uMCB in loss
  units is not comparable across assets without normalisation. That is a
  presentational advantage, and it is worth one sentence, not a contribution.
- **Do not claim regime (b).** The rank divergence in Panel A is real, but it is
  explained by the density term, not by q̂_V capturing anything uMCB misses.
  Presenting it as a discovered regime would be exactly the disguised novelty
  claim the brief warns against.

## The finding that is worth more than the verdict

The decomposition also measures how much of the miscalibration a constant shift
can reach at all. Across Panel A — the forecasters that are actually usable —
the unconditional share is **median 0.354**: a constant shift addresses about a
third of total miscalibration, and two thirds is conditional and untouched by
any version of this method.

That is a sharper statement of the method's limit than the paper currently
makes, it is measured rather than argued, and it converges with the Phase 1
result (the correction is close to free where it is not needed) and with the
duration tests (clustering the scalar shift cannot remove). All three say the
same thing: **the instrument is unconditional, and the residual problem is
conditional.**

The decomposition identity holds to 1.6e-16, so the implementation is sound.

## What was implemented, for the record

Isotonic quantile regression by pool-adjacent-violators with the weighted
α-quantile as the pooling operation — the CORP recalibration of Gneiting & Resin
specialised to the quantile loss, since sklearn's isotonic routine minimises
squared error and is not applicable. Outcome (c) does not arise: uMCB is
computable here without additional assumptions.
