# Table 1's "CC pass" column: what it counts

Generated by `analysis/cc_column/investigate_cc.py`.

## The rule

**The column counts a degenerate test as a pass**, and that reproduces **10 of 10** published rows exactly.

| Model | degenerate (NaN) | informative pass (p>0.05) | sum | Table 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moirai-1.1 | 18 | 6 | **24** | **24** |
| Lag-Llama | 22 | 2 | **24** | **24** |
| GJR-GARCH | 13 | 7 | **20** | **20** |
| GARCH-N | 12 | 6 | **18** | **18** |
| Hist-Sim | 8 | 6 | **14** | **14** |
| EWMA | 14 | 7 | **21** | **21** |
| Chronos-Small | 11 | 4 | **15** | **15** |
| Chronos-Mini | 11 | 5 | **16** | **16** |
| TimesFM-2.5 | 10 | 0 | **10** | **10** |
| Moirai-2.0 | 7 | 0 | **7** | **7** |

With roughly 15 violations in ~1500 test days, most pairs have **no consecutive violations at all** (n11 = n10 = 0). The Christoffersen independence LR is then undefined, the pipeline stores NaN, and the table counts it as a pass.

This is not a coding slip to be silently repaired — it is a convention, and the published numbers are arithmetically correct under it. But it is not evidence of independence. It is the absence of any information about independence.

## What it does to the paper's claim

The text states that among Panel A pairs passing Kupiec, 86.1% also pass Christoffersen, against 40.6% in Panel B, and reads this as evidence that the regimes differ in conditional coverage.

| | Panel A | Panel B |
|---|---|---|
| Pairs passing Kupiec | 108 | 32 |
| **Published rule** (NaN = pass) | 93/108 = **86.1%** | 13/32 = **40.6%** |
| of which degenerate (no consecutive violations) | 69/108 = 63.9% | 10/32 = 31.2% |
| **Informative pairs only** | 24/39 = **61.5%** | 3/22 = **13.6%** |

The published percentages reproduce exactly (86.1% and 40.6%), so the arithmetic is right. But **64% of Panel A's "passes" carry no information**, and the same is true of 31% in Panel B.

## The good news

**The qualitative finding survives, and in ratio terms it gets stronger.** Restricted to pairs where the test is actually defined, Panel A passes 61.5% of the time against 13.6% for Panel B — a 4.5x gap, against 2.1x under the published rule. The regimes do differ in conditional coverage; the headline percentages were just inflated by the convention.

## What has to change in the text

1. Report the informative subset (24/39 vs 3/22) as the claim, and the published-rule numbers only alongside an explicit statement of the convention.
2. State the convention wherever the CC column appears: a pair with no consecutive violations is recorded as a pass because the test is undefined, not because independence was demonstrated.
3. Report the degeneracy rate itself (64% of Panel A, 31% of Panel B). It is a substantive finding: **the Christoffersen independence test has almost no power at α = 0.01 in samples of this length.** That is the honest answer to Referee 1's point (viii) and the AE's point 1 — the paper cannot claim conditional-coverage evidence from this test at the 1% level, and should say so rather than report a number that looks like evidence.

Point 3 is worth more to the paper than the original claim was. It converts a weak result into a measured statement about what the standard backtest can and cannot detect in the extreme tail — which is the same identification-constraint argument the paper already makes for estimation, now extended to testing.
